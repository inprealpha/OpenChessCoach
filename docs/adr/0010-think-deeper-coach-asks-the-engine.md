# Think deeper: the Coach can ask the Engine while it answers

The brief (ADR 0004, ADR 0009) covers the position, the Engine's best moves, the move under discussion and the replies to it. A Player studying a position often wants more: what an alternative leads to two moves later, or why the natural-looking move fails. Without more Engine facts the Coach either says nothing about these or works the chess out itself, which ADR 0002 forbids. So the Player can turn on **Think deeper** in Settings. In a Why Question or a Review key moment, the Coach then gets one tool, `ask_engine`. It names a position by a line of SAN moves from the brief's position, and optionally one move there. The desktop's Engine answers with what a brief would say: its best moves there with their Move Grades, lines, `does` and `result`, and for the move its Move Grade against the Engine's best, its line, its facts and the Engine's replies. For a line, it also says what the line's last move does, as the brief says what the Player's move does, so the Coach never has to work that out from the facts on the moves after it. The answer is built by the same code that builds the brief (`packages/shared/src/coach/engine-tool.ts`), so the Coach reads the Engine's verdicts and never a raw line it has to interpret.

## How it relates to the earlier decisions

- **ADR 0001 (one owner, the owner's own Claude login, no general tools).** The tool is an in-process MCP server inside the backend. It runs nothing, reads no file and opens no connection; it can only call the backend's `EngineService`. The Coach still has no built-in tools (`tools: []`), no other MCP server (`strictMcpConfig`), and no settings from disk, and only this tool's exact name is allowed to run.
- **ADR 0002 (the Engine judges, the Coach explains).** Every answer is the Engine's judgement. The move guard now counts a move from the backend's own answers in that call as Engine output. A move played in the board's own position (the brief's position, or after the move) may be drawn; a move deeper in a line may only be named, as with an asked move's line. The moves of a line the Coach sends are its question, not the Engine's answer, so they are not accepted unless an answer also has them. A move the Coach makes up is stripped as before.
- **ADR 0004 (lean Coach calls).** The brief and its budget are unchanged, and every call still starts fresh. The tool only adds tokens inside the one call, and a cap bounds them: at most 3 answered questions per answer. On a first question the Coach is told to ask at least once: told only to ask when the brief falls short, the latest Haiku never asked in the first 10 positions, because the brief always looks complete to it. On a follow-up (the brief has a summary), asking is the Coach's call, since the thread may already have covered it. A question past the cap is refused with a message telling the Coach to answer from what it has, so the Player still gets an answer. A question with an illegal move, or one the Engine can't answer, is refused with the reason and doesn't use up a question; up to 2 such refusals have turns of their own, so they can't leave the Coach without a turn to answer in. Text the Coach writes before asking the Engine is thinking aloud: the Player never sees it and the reply never keeps it. While a question runs, the Coach panel says so ("The Coach is checking with the Engine (question 1)…"), and the answer streams as soon as the Coach's turn moves on to it, or as soon as no question is left to ask. When a call ends, cancelled or stopped at its deadline, the Engine searches its questions started are stopped or dropped, so the shared desktop Engine never works for an answer nobody reads. Two more ceilings bound a call that goes wrong: every Coach response, with the tool or without, may have at most 8,000 output tokens, and an answer with the tool at most 90 seconds, after which the call stops and the Player is told to ask again or turn Think deeper off. A Review's key moments share three minutes for the tool: each gets the smaller of 90 seconds and what is left, the moments after that answer from the brief alone, and a moment that runs out of time answers once more from the brief alone rather than failing. Normal answers stay under 4,000 output tokens and 25 seconds; in the comparison, one answer ran away to 130,000 output tokens over ten minutes, the model repeating itself until its own limit, twice. Hints, Live Coaching and Lesson turns never get the tool: Hints and Live Coaching must stay short, and Lesson turns are judged against their own notes and Move Grades (ADR 0008). Think deeper is off by default.
- **ADR 0007 (the backend computes every Engine fact).** Only the backend builds the tool, from its own Engine, and only when the setting stored on the desktop is on. A client can't switch it on for one request, and nothing a client sends adds to the moves the guard accepts. The tests send a forged setting and forged answers and check that both are ignored.
- **ADR 0009 (facts, not lines).** The answers carry the same `does` and `result` facts, under the same three rules, so the tool can't say anything the brief couldn't. The tool adds Engine searches, which the brief avoids: one search for the position, and one more for a move the Engine didn't list.

## Consequences

- Answers with Think deeper on take longer and use more of the owner's Claude limits. The Settings screen says so, and says the setting is meant for deeper study.
- **Think deeper makes answers more complete, not more accurate, on every model measured.** The Coach answered a fixed set of 25 positions (20 for tuning, 5 held out) with it off and on, from the same brief; Opus judges checked every claim with the Engine at depth 18, without knowing which mode wrote which answer. With the final code (one Sonnet group of 5 positions left unjudged):

  | Off → on | Haiku (25) | Sonnet (20) | Opus (25) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | False claims | 9 → 14 | 2 → 3 | 3 → 5 |
  | Answers with a false claim | 9 → 8 | 2 → 3 | 2 → 5 |
  | Answers missing the main reason | 3 → 2 | 1 → 0 | 3 → 0 |
  | Question fully answered | 18 → 18 | 13 → 18 | 17 → 20 |
  | Median time per answer | 10.7 → 15.1 s | 6.3 → 9.8 s | 14.9 → 15.4 s |
  | Median input tokens (mostly cached) | 3,900 → 11,000 | 3,850 → 10,650 | 3,850 → 10,850 |

  The same answers judged twice moved by up to 3 out of 20, so only the larger differences mean much. Before the last fix, Sonnet's false claims doubled with the tool on (6 → 12): it described the last move of its own question line from the facts on the moves after it, and added up material over that line. Giving that move its own facts (`lastMove`) and telling the Coach that facts belong to their own move brought Sonnet back to level. Most remaining false claims, in both modes, are the Coach's own wording: a reply called forced when it is only best, or material added up over a line.
- On Haiku, Think deeper added false claims (9 → 14 over 25 positions) while making answers fuller, so it keeps working there, but the Think deeper card in Settings says so whenever Why Questions or Reviews run on Haiku, as with the Thrifty preset (the owner's call). Balanced and Best, on Sonnet and Opus, show no warning.
- The questions and the Engine's answers are logged with each Coach call (the `coach_questions` table), also when the call ends at the usage limit or with the Coach unavailable, and the Coach panel says how many questions an answer asked.
- The real-Coach check makes one Why Question with Think deeper on, and accepts moves from the Engine's answers exactly as the guard does.
- The fake Coach asks one question, or scripted ones, so verify mode and the tests exercise the tool end to end.
