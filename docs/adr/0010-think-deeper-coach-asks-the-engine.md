# Think deeper: the Coach can ask the Engine while it answers

The brief (ADR 0004, ADR 0009) covers the position, the Engine's best moves, the move under discussion and the replies to it. A Player studying a position often wants more: what an alternative leads to two moves later, or why the natural-looking move fails. Without more Engine facts the Coach either says nothing about these or works the chess out itself, which ADR 0002 forbids. So the Player can turn on **Think deeper** in Settings. In a Why Question or a Review key moment, the Coach then gets one tool, `ask_engine`. It names a position by a line of SAN moves from the brief's position, and optionally one move there. The desktop's Engine answers with what a brief would say: its best moves there with their Move Grades, lines, `does` and `result`, and for the move its Move Grade against the Engine's best, its line, its facts and the Engine's replies. The answer is built by the same code that builds the brief (`packages/shared/src/coach/engine-tool.ts`), so the Coach reads the Engine's verdicts and never a raw line it has to interpret.

## How it relates to the earlier decisions

- **ADR 0001 (one owner, the owner's own Claude login, no general tools).** The tool is an in-process MCP server inside the backend. It runs nothing, reads no file and opens no connection; it can only call the backend's `EngineService`. The Coach still has no built-in tools (`tools: []`), no other MCP server (`strictMcpConfig`), and no settings from disk, and only this tool's exact name is allowed to run.
- **ADR 0002 (the Engine judges, the Coach explains).** Every answer is the Engine's judgement. The move guard now counts a move from the backend's own answers in that call as Engine output. A move played in the board's own position (the brief's position, or after the move) may be drawn; a move deeper in a line may only be named, as with an asked move's line. The moves of a line the Coach sends are its question, not the Engine's answer, so they are not accepted unless an answer also has them. A move the Coach makes up is stripped as before.
- **ADR 0004 (lean Coach calls).** The brief and its budget are unchanged, and every call still starts fresh. The tool only adds tokens inside the one call, and a cap bounds them: at most 3 answered questions per answer. The Coach is told to ask at least once: told only to ask when the brief falls short, the latest Haiku never asked in the first 10 positions, because the brief always looks complete to it. A question past the cap is refused with a message telling the Coach to answer from what it has, so the Player still gets an answer. A question with an illegal move is refused with the reason and doesn't use up a question. The call allows one more turn per question than without the tool. Hints, Live Coaching and Lesson turns never get the tool: Hints and Live Coaching must stay short, and Lesson turns are judged against their own notes and Move Grades (ADR 0008). Think deeper is off by default.
- **ADR 0007 (the backend computes every Engine fact).** Only the backend builds the tool, from its own Engine, and only when the setting stored on the desktop is on. A client can't switch it on for one request, and nothing a client sends adds to the moves the guard accepts. The tests send a forged setting and forged answers and check that both are ignored.
- **ADR 0009 (facts, not lines).** The answers carry the same `does` and `result` facts, under the same three rules, so the tool can't say anything the brief couldn't. The tool adds Engine searches, which the brief avoids: one search for the position, and one more for a move the Engine didn't list.

## Consequences

- Answers with Think deeper on take longer and use more of the owner's Claude limits. The Settings screen says so, and says the setting is meant for deeper study.
- The questions and the Engine's answers are logged with each Coach call (the `coach_questions` table), and the Coach panel says how many questions an answer asked.
- The real-Coach check makes one Why Question with Think deeper on, and accepts moves from the Engine's answers exactly as the guard does.
- The fake Coach asks one question, or scripted ones, so verify mode and the tests exercise the tool end to end.
